# Cultural context and source limits

Research and owner review: 11 October 2026. Sources inform selected regional concepts, not a universal image of Assam. No online photograph was downloaded or directly included as a generation reference. Existing owner-approved generated samples are the style references.

Official Rang Ghar and Talatal Ghar photographs were inspected in the browser for visual checks, without downloading or embedding them in the collection. The Rang Ghar revision also used the existing owner-supplied brand artwork as a silhouette reference; that artwork's upstream rights remain a separate dependency. Visual checks: https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/inline-images/rangghar2_0.jpg ; https://sivasagar.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/inline-images/Talatol_b.jpg . The Talatal photograph is an aerial overview, not a measured record of the generated corridor fragment.

## Housing

Sample 01 proposed a present-day Brahmaputra Valley Assam-type home: low masonry plinth, timber framing, pale infill walls, wooden shutters and corrugated roofing. Angsuman accepted its architecture, exterior and vegetation, then authorised the full collection. Ikra is a reed material associated with lightweight regional construction; an illustration's plastered surface cannot prove its concealed material. Raised chang ghar housing belongs to a distinct floodplain/community context and was not treated as universal rural housing.

Research lead: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cscm.2025.e04461 (indexed abstract/excerpt available; full text fetch blocked). Floodplain distinction: https://www.onlineasdma.assam.gov.in/kmp/public/uploads/content/1741041418.pdf (indexed excerpt, full PDF not inspected).

## Valleys, wetland and hills

The Water Resources Department describes the Brahmaputra's braided character in Assam. AE-01 uses channels and sandbars without reproducing a map. AE-06 draws on Barak Valley's river/alluvial setting and uses a different composition; it is not a specific verified river bend.

Sources: https://waterresources.assam.gov.in/portlet-innerpage/brahmaputra-river-system ; https://waterresources.assam.gov.in/portlet-innerpage/barak-river-system ; https://cachar.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/public_utility/District%20Disaster%20Management%20Plan%20%28DDMP%202023-24%29.pdf (indexed district excerpt).

The Ramsar information sheet describes Deepor Beel as a freshwater lake linked to a former river channel. AE-03 is a general freshwater-beel concept, not a literal depiction of Deepor Beel or a current biodiversity survey. Source: https://rsis.ramsar.org/RISapp/files/RISrep/IN1207RIS.pdf (indexed excerpt).

The Dima Hasao district site informs the hill-region setting. AE-05 avoids alpine/snowy imagery and exact community costume; its settlement layout is a creative hypothesis. Source: https://dimahasao.assam.gov.in/ .

## Tea and work

Tea Board materials locate Assam tea production and list shade-tree planting material for the region. AE-02 uses low pruned tea rows with shade trees, avoiding a generic steep mountain plantation. Sources: https://www.teaboard.gov.in/TEABOARDCSM/Ng%3D%3D ; https://teaboard.gov.in/pdf/LIST_OF_APPROVED_PLANTING_MATERIALS_AND_SOIL_TESTING_LABORATORIES_pdf2583.pdf (indexed excerpts).

Government procurement records document wooden frame/fly-shuttle looms. EL-02 is a weaving-workshop context illustration, not an equipment assembly guide or proof of a particular textile tradition. Technical specification reference: https://www.handlooms.gov.in/assets/img/tenders/EOI.pdf (indexed excerpt); Assam tender portal: https://assamtenders.gov.in/nicgep/app .

Other livelihoods are plausible everyday scenarios, not ethnographic records. No claim that every farmer, family, vendor or enterprise uses the same building, clothing or tools. Generated household details require contextual judgment when pairing art with a specific community lesson.
